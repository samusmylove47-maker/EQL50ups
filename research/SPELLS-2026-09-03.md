# Spells — what exists, what we hold, what is still missing

Written 3 September 2026, after the owner's instruction:

> *"We **NEED** this information. I'm going to request that you or another session does an
> exhaustive search/scrape/etc for this information from other logs or repos or wiki or
> websites, etc. Another session, a local session, needs to scan through the game's files
> and make a definitive determination if there's any spell information that can be gathered
> from these local files on my machine."*

Every count below names the command that produced it.

---

## 1. The first answer is that we already had it

Four spell datasets have been vendored in `research/data/` since **16 August** and are read
by nothing at all — not the pipeline, not the app, not a test.

| file | holds |
|---|---|
| `jmoyers-spells.json` | **1,928 spells**, effects parsed on 1,927, scraped 2026-08-12 |
| `jmoyers-class-spells.json` | 13 classes, spells with levels |
| `jmoyers-spelllines-merged.json` | 13 classes, spell lines with cross-checks |
| `eqbuddy-harvest-spells.json` | 1,929 records, an independent second harvest |

`node pipeline/probe-spells.mjs` produces those figures.

**`Call of Flame` — the spell the request named — is in all four.** The vendored record:

```
Call of Flame · Ranger 49 · 300 damage · 106 mana · 0.50s cast
Instant · Single · Detrimental · "You feel your skin ignite."
```

So the problem was never acquisition. It was that nothing reads them.

## 2. What we had was half a record

Checked against the live wiki, the vendored `Call of Flame` is **correct and incomplete**.
Every number it carries matches the wiki exactly. What it does not carry:

| field | wiki | in the vendored corpus |
|---|---|---|
| recast time | **10.00 s** | absent |
| range | 200 | absent |
| fizzle time | 2.50 s | absent |
| resist | Fire (0) | absent |
| skill | Evocation | absent |
| how you obtain it | Call of Flame Quest | absent |
| era | `{{Classic Era}}` | absent |

**Recast time is not a nice-to-have for this project.** A 300-damage nuke on a 10-second
recast and a 300-damage nuke on a 2-second recast are different spells, and a tool that
ranks damage cannot tell them apart without it. The vendored corpus cannot rank sustained
damage at all.

### Two data-quality findings in that table

**`Shadow Knight` and `Shadowknight` are both used**, 76 spells against 15. That is the
wiki's own inconsistency, not a parsing artefact — the two spellings are literally what the
pages say. The owner confirmed they are the same class, and `wiki-spells.mjs` now folds
them at the point of reading, so the class shows its true **91 spells, 72 at the cap**
rather than 76. Every other class name passes through untouched: this is a correction of
one known collision, not a normaliser guessing at names it has not seen.

**The corpus runs past this game's level cap.** Spells are recorded up to level 60, and one
Paladin entry to 63, against a cap of 50. So a spell list needs the same level and era
gating the item catalogue already has — 1,177 of 1,455 class-attributed spells are castable
at 50 or below.

## 4. Other EQL sites — three of them are off limits

Found by web search on 3 September, then each site's `robots.txt` fetched and read before
anything else was touched.

|---|---|---|
| **eqlwiki.com** | 2,011 | open — the source everything else derives from |
| **eqlbase.com** | 1,448 (self-reported, updated 28 Aug) | `Allow: /` |
| **eqlbuildforge.com** | searchable spell reference | `Allow: /` |
| **eql-tools.com** | spell search + "mote upgrades" | `Allow: /` with path exclusions |
| **gnollguard.com** | spell DB by class | **`ClaudeBot: Disallow: /`** |
| **eqlegends.tools** | spellbook by class and level | **`ClaudeBot: Disallow: /`** |
| **eqprogression.com** | Legends spells section | **`ClaudeBot: Disallow: /`** |

**Three sites refuse us by name and were not crawled.** Beyond `robots.txt` nothing was
fetched from any of them.

One of those is awkward and should be recorded plainly: **`eqprogression.com` is named in
`research/SOURCING-STANDARD.md` as a Tier 3 source**, and it now disallows `ClaudeBot`.
Our own standard cites a source this project may not read automatically. That is not a
crisis — a person may read it — but the standard should say so rather than implying a
route that is closed.

### Spells DO upgrade — Rank 0 to Rank 10 — and the wiki does not know it

This section recorded the mote/rank ladder as unverified, because the wiki does not
corroborate it at all:

```
eqlwiki search "mote spell upgrade" -> 0 hits
pages Mote / Motes / Spell Motes    -> all missing
Template:SpellSlotRowSmart          -> a display switch, not a tier table
```

The owner settled it on 3 September:

> *"Spells do upgrade from Rank 0 to Rank 10. They don't just gain damage, or reduce cast
> time, or cooldown time, or resistance penetration, or increased duration... usually it is
> a mix of many things."*

So the wiki's silence was a gap in the wiki, not evidence of absence — and this project
would have modelled spells wrongly by trusting it.

## 4a. `amerzel/eql-info` — the rank model, already reverse-engineered

The owner pointed at <https://amerzel.github.io/eql-info/>, an **EQL Spell Explorer**.
It is **MIT licensed**, © 2026 James Whiteneck, source at `github.com/amerzel/eql-info`,
and it carries a worked model of exactly the mechanic above in `static/js/upgrades.js`:

> *"The tier system is SERVER-PUSHED: none of this lives in `spells_us.txt`. Rates below
> are reverse-engineered from n=88 tooltip observations."*

Category-based scaling, per rank:

| category | cast | mana | duration | confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nuke / lifetap | −2% | −2% | — | solid |
| DoT | −4% | −2% | +5% | solid |
| heal | −4% | −2% | — | solid |
| heal over time | −4% | −2% | +5% | duration inferred |
| debuff | −4% | −4% | +10% | duration inferred |
| charm / mez | −4% | −4% | +10% | solid |
| buff | −4% | −4% | +10% | solid |

Universal, whatever the category: **recovery −2%/rank** (nearest 0.1s, ties down),
**reuse −2%/rank** (floor-truncated, hard floor 1s), **resist penetration `base − 15×rank`**.
Instant and Permanent durations are exempt. Damage is tooltip-hidden and combat-observed
only: **`floor(base × (1 + 0.06×rank))`** for nukes, 0.03 for DoTs, heals ~+3%/rank from a
single datapoint.

**And the cost to advance a rank is `2^rank` motes.**

That last line is the one to notice. This project's *item* upgrade model already computes
`fractionDenominator(full) = 2 ** full` — the same doubling ladder, derived independently
from item tooltips months earlier. Two separate reverse-engineerings of two separate
systems landing on the same progression curve is meaningful corroboration that both are
right.

Two further rules recorded there, both attributed to in-game observation: a proc'd spell
inherits **half** its parent's rank (`floor(N/2)`), and each rank raises a charm spell's
target-level cap by **+1**.

`TIER_MAX = 10` is annotated *"reagent-skip patch note implies cap 10; unverified"* — the
owner's report now confirms it.

**Nothing here is Tier M for us.** It is a named community tool with its own stated
confidences, which under `research/SOURCING-STANDARD.md` is Tier 3 — and unusually good
Tier 3, because it distinguishes "solid" from "inferred" per field the way this project
does. It is a cross-check, not a substitute for reading the game.

## 5. What is still missing

- **Whether spells upgrade at all.** §4. Unresolved and consequential.
- **593 spells state no era**, the same unplaced problem the item catalogue has.
- **No spell data ships.** The payload is items only; this vendored corpus is a research
  artefact until something reads it.
- **Nothing is Tier M.** Every figure here is Tier 2 — a wiki page somebody typed. No spell
  in this project has been checked against a client window, the way five item stat blocks
  and now four Shadow Rage pieces have.

## 6. What a local session should look for — now a much shorter list

The owner: *"Spell data is readily available."* It is, and `amerzel/eql-info`'s README and
`SPELL_FORMAT.md` say exactly where and in what shape, so this no longer needs discovering.

**The files, in the EQL client install:**

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `spells_us.txt` | **73,915 spells**, 171 caret-delimited columns, no header |
| `spells_us_str.txt` | the five message-text columns, split out |
| `dbstr_us.txt` | description strings with placeholders the spell values fill |
| `uifiles/default/Spells01..63.tga` | spell gem icons |

`SPELL_FORMAT.md` in that repo is a 380-line reverse-engineered spec of the layout. Its
headline finding: **EQL's format is the modern Live EverQuest format plus exactly five
trailing fields**, with ~2,961 more spells than Live carries — i.e. EQL's own custom
content is identifiable as the delta. Columns 0–164 are bitwise identical to Live for 99%+
of shared spells.

For scale: the client file holds **73,915** spells against the wiki's **2,011**. Most of
that is NPC and internal content, but the player-castable subset is certainly larger and
certainly more accurate than anything scraped.

**What the files will NOT answer, and this is the important caveat:** the rank ladder is
**server-pushed and absent from `spells_us.txt`** (§4a). No amount of file reading recovers
it. It has to come from tooltip observation in a live client — which is what James did for
n=88 spells, and what this project's own Tier M captures do for items.

So the local session's job splits cleanly:

1. **Parse the client files** for the base spell corpus. MIT-licensed parser and format
   spec already exist; nothing needs inventing.
2. **Capture ranked tooltips** for a sample of spells across categories, to check the §4a
   rates rather than adopt them. The same discipline as `TIER0-VALIDATION.md`: predict from
   the model, compare against the window, record the count.

A parsed `spells_us.txt` plus a dozen ranked tooltips would put spells on the same footing
as items — and would be the first Tier M spell data this project has ever had.
